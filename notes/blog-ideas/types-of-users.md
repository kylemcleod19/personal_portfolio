# Who are the true power users? The 100Xers?

## Background

I went to the Claude meetup last night (4/13), hosted by Anthropic. There were probably 100 people there with varying levels of experience.

One thing I noticed. There were many people with deep cuirousity around AI itself. How to optimize tokens, how to setup agent harnesses, how to deploy agents and ensure you get the goals from your agents. They were actively practicing how to make their AI approaches more efficient with tools like harnesses, context management approaches, and agent management. They referenced being able to give their agentic programs a single prompt and get back a full platform after it worked overnight.

There was one presenter who was using relatively crude AI agent architecture (relative to the audience - probably pretty advanced over a non-user) but he had a solid business idea and had been working on how to get his ideas out in front of real users. He was solving problems like how do you get lawyers to trust AI processing? What key processes can I turn over to AI and which ones do I need to put in front of the human. He had a powerful partnership with expert humans doing critical tasks and AI doing rote data handling and large-scale analysis. He was embedded in the customers work space.

He fielded a lot of questions that sounded something like "are you using [insert innovative scaling, harnessing, agent-structuring, API etc. AI practice]?" and he would respond with something like "I haven't found a use for that".

## Initial Takeaways

I came away thinking two things:

1. The presenter who had spent more time solving customer problems seemed like he had gotten the most value out of AI. He could clearly communicate the value it solved for a valuable target market. He had spent time tailoring his approach to his market to increase adoption.
2. The presenter who was solving cusotmer problems could likely benefit from the expertise of others in the room at some point. There are a lot of good ideas.
3. Will Claude and other AI companies figure out how to incorporate AI best practices and tricks into their platforms? If so knowing these things is much less important.

## What it means

### The Axes of AI knowledge

I think of the AI practitioners along two axes:
1. Ability to apply AI to a real problem with real customers. Do they know a business space/problem well? Do they have an idea as to where AI adds the most leverage? Are they validating with real customers? Do they have a real business case?
2. Ability to build more independent AIs. Can my AI efficiently do work without any human in the loop? Does it have mechanisms to improve itself? Can I use less tokens?

I think there are a lot of people using pretty simple approaches to solve more basic and personal problems. These would be low on both axes. There are a few high on the real problems axis and low-medium on the AI sophistocation axis. There are more on the high sofisticaiton axis and lower on the business problem axis. There are very few high in both axis.

### The value of AI knowledge

The importance of AI sophistication was not important in the presenters case becuase he was working with lawyers and a relatively low AI transaction volume. Bulk inputs, processed a few times a week, with a high impact per transaction (reducing law/paralegal wages). This highlights the disparate value of sophistication/automation depending on use case.

Smaller and more frequent transactions reqiure more automation and sofistication. You don't want to have to manually run and evaluate transactions if your volume is high and/or your value per transaction is low. You need an AI that can determine if it worked, be cost efficient, etc. Here being able to use more sophisticated approaches is highly valuable and can flip the profitability of your AI business case.

There is a risk though in learning sophisticated techniques as the AI providers will likely roll many of them into their platform offerings. Only the bleeding edge users are likely to be ahead of the platform (claude code, etc).

### What should you do?

#### Keep Building

Build for whatever business case you have. If you build something personal, think about "What would another user group think about this?" and expand to that. Learn how to build for real problems and think about a full cusotmer use case.

#### Base Learning Activities on Findings

When you build ask yourself questions to help guide your learning.

| Guiding Questions | What it will help |
| ---------------|-------------------|
| What do real users have problems with? | Refine what you should be building. Help you approach real problems and not just ones tailored for AI 
| How do they find the AI experiences I am creating? | Learn how to add AI into your UX. |
 Where in my vibe coding am I struggling? | Focus learning new AI techniques on real problems. |

## Most B2B Software Is Generic by Necessity

For most of the last 20 years, the SaaS model worked like this: build once, sell to many. The economics required it. Custom software was expensive. The more a vendor could standardize their product, the more customers they could serve without proportionally growing their engineering team.

That constraint shaped everything. Most B2B buyers learned to accept software that almost fit. They hired consultants to bridge the gap. They built spreadsheets to handle edge cases. They paid for features they didn't use and worked around the ones they needed.

## What Customers Want

Take Company B, the job shop. Nearly every job is quoted fresh, and capacity is daily triage across unlike machines. The best off-the-shelf product it can buy was built around a steady production schedule instead.

<ShopFitRadar client:visible mode="want" />

A closer fit does two things. It covers the capabilities the shop actually runs on, here quoting and capacity planning. And it drops the ones it doesn't: a job shop has little use for deep traceability or formal quality documentation, but a general product charges for them anyway. Customers want the features they need, without paying for the ones they don't.

### How They Fill the Gap Today

When the software falls short, the need doesn't go away. It moves into Airtable, Retool and spreadsheets.

<ShopFitRadar client:visible mode="gap" />

At a $50M ARR manufacturing marketplace, we replaced purpose-built SaaS tools with Retool and Airtable workflows. Not because we preferred them. Because the off-the-shelf options didn't fit, and these let us build something closer to what we actually needed without a full engineering cycle.

The result was clumsy. Retool requires a developer. Airtable breaks at scale. The UX is obviously stitched together. But we got workflows that matched our operations instead of the reverse.

Companies are already cobbling together whatever tools they can make work to fill in gaps left by under-verticalized SaaS. These tools prove the need and show a marker ripe for a better solution.

### AI Changes the Build Economics

The cost to build software has dropped and is continuing to drop. Customer challenges are solvable at the right cost. As industries get closer to those costs, new solutions will be built, and we will see hyper-verticalized SaaS where solutions get closer to smaller segments of customers.

What will happen:
  
1. The market will produce more tailored SaaS solutions
1. Gap fillers (airtable and retool in above example) will be abandoned
1. SaaS covering generic needs like accounting and sales will do fine

I am not sure when it will happen or in what verticals it will start, but I am confident we will see this change.

### Proving it

I am testing out this trend in hypothesis in two ways. Firt, I have been consulting for companies like my examples on their SaaS software solutions. I have already seen companies eager to abandon a suite of tools for a hyper-verticalized option.

Second, I am vibe coding and building the types of software I see companies desiring. I am finding out how quickly we can go from ideas to performant solutions and what techniques get us there. I have been excited by what I have been able to build, and I think this is a great future for builders and customers alike.
